## 1. Executive Summary

**DealMarket Cameroon** is a two-sided AI Retail Intelligence Platform connecting Cameroonian consumers with supermarket promotions, and giving supermarkets an AI-powered command center to plan, execute, and measure retail marketing. It consists of a consumer mobile app (Flutter), a retailer web platform (Next.js), and a centralized AI backend that powers deal ranking, OCR flyer ingestion, marketing content generation, and retail business intelligence.

This is a commercial SaaS product, not a prototype. It is designed from day one to scale across Cameroon and expand into additional African markets without major architectural rework.

## 2. Problem Statement

**Consumers** in Cameroon have no single place to discover and compare supermarket promotions. Deals are scattered across printed flyers, WhatsApp forwards, and individual store visits, making price comparison slow and inefficient, and causing consumers to miss savings.

**Supermarkets** — from national chains to independent stores — have limited digital tools to reach price-sensitive shoppers, plan promotional calendars around Cameroonian cultural and commercial events, understand which products and campaigns actually drive results, or compete effectively with each other on evolving digital channels. Flyer creation and distribution is manual, slow, and disconnected from any measurement of outcomes.

There is no existing platform in Cameroon that combines consumer deal discovery with AI-driven retail intelligence for merchants.

### 6.3 AI OCR Engine

Automatically ingests PDF catalogs, flyers, images, and scanned brochures, extracting per product: image, name, brand, category, barcode (if present), original price, promotional price, discount percentage, promotion period, store, quantity, packaging, and a confidence score. All extracted data requires **human verification before publishing**.

### 6.4 Smart Deal Ranking

An AI ranking algorithm determines the "best deal" surfaced to each consumer, using: distance, discount, price, popularity, user preferences, shopping history, season, product availability, sponsored priority, store rating, recent searches, and trending products.

### 6.5 Retail Business Intelligence

The web platform functions as an **AI Retail Command Center**, continuously answering: what to promote, when, and how; which products are trending or losing engagement; which categories are growing; which campaigns delivered the highest ROI; which promotions should be extended; and how the retailer compares to competitors — without exposing confidential competitor data.

### 6.6 Cameroon Retail Knowledge Engine

The AI understands Cameroonian commercial and cultural calendar behavior and proactively prepares campaigns **2–6 weeks in advance** for: New Year, Youth Day, Valentine's Day, Women's Day, Ramadan, Easter, Labour Day, National Day, Back-to-School, Black Friday, Christmas, New Year's Eve, Mother's Day, Father's Day, Eid, and Lent. For each event it recommends products, discounts, campaign names, push notification copy, advertising budget, sponsorship suggestions, flyers, and social copy, campaign duration, target audience, and timing.

### 6.7 AI Marketing Assistant

Auto-generates, in French and English: Facebook and Instagram campaigns/captions, WhatsApp and SMS campaigns, TikTok captions, email campaigns, push notification copy, store posters, flyers, promotional banners, slogans, calls-to-action, and ad copy.

### 6.8 AI Competition Analysis

Compares participating supermarkets on best prices, most-viewed products, trending categories, engagement, price competitiveness, and promotion effectiveness — surfacing recommendations to each retailer without exposing another retailer's confidential data.

### 6.9 Customer Insights

Analyzes searches, favorites, shares, wishlist activity, clicks, location, season, and shopping behavior to recommend what each retailer should promote next.

### 6.10 Business Intelligence Dashboard

Executive dashboards covering views, clicks, shares, favorites, followers, conversion estimates, ROI, a promotion health score, AI insights, top-performing categories and stores, weekly/monthly/seasonal trends, and forecasts — with downloadable executive reports.

## 12. Assumptions & Constraints

- **Connectivity & devices**: Significant share of users are on mid/low-end Android devices with variable mobile data quality — offline caching and lightweight payloads are a hard requirement, not a nice-to-have.
- **Bilingual by default**: French and English are both first-class languages throughout the consumer app, admin portal, and all AI-generated content — not a translation layer bolted on later.
- **Mobile money is primary**: MTN Mobile Money and Orange Money are the dominant payment methods in-market and must be treated as first-class, not secondary to card payments.
- **Flyer-driven retail culture**: Much of Cameroonian retail promotion still happens via printed/PDF flyers — the OCR pipeline is not a convenience feature, it is core to retailer onboarding at scale.
- **Human-in-the-loop AI**: All AI-extracted or AI-generated content that reaches consumers or represents pricing (OCR extraction, generated marketing copy) requires human verification before publishing, to protect data accuracy and brand trust.
- **Multi-tenancy from day one**: Data model, auth, and admin tooling must assume many independent retailer organizations with multiple branches and staff roles, even though early launch will have a small partner set.
- **Designed for expansion**: No architectural decision should assume Cameroon-only; country/currency/language/tax configuration must be data-driven, not hardcoded.

## 13. Out of Scope (Initial Release)

- E-commerce checkout / online ordering (this is a deal-discovery and marketing platform, not a marketplace, at launch)
- In-app delivery logistics
- WhatsApp Business API notification channel (planned, not launch)
- Non-supermarket retail verticals (pharmacies, electronics, fashion) — architecture should not preclude this later, but it is not in scope now

## 14. Key Risks

| Risk | Mitigation direction |
|---|---|
| Low retailer digital literacy slows onboarding | OCR-first onboarding path requiring minimal manual data entry; guided flows |
| OCR accuracy on low-quality scanned flyers | Confidence scoring + mandatory human verification step before publishing |
| Mobile money payment integration reliability | Abstract payment providers behind a common interface; monitor and alert on failures |
| Data cost sensitivity limits consumer engagement | Aggressive caching, image optimization, offline-first UX |
| Competitive/confidential data leakage between retailers | Strict aggregation and anonymization rules in the competition-analysis engine |
| Scope breadth risks slow time-to-market | Phased MVP/Wave delivery (Section 11) with explicit approval gates per phase |

## 15. Expansion Strategy

The platform must be designed so that adding a new country requires **configuration, not architectural change**: new currency, language, payment providers, tax rules, and a localized commercial calendar (the "Retail Knowledge Engine" is inherently country-specific and must be a pluggable data layer, not hardcoded logic). This principle governs decisions in every subsequent phase, starting with system architecture and database schema.
